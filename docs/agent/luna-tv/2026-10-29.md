## Current Android TV implementation

- Foreground Android TV / Google TV GLES demo. This is not transparent or system-wide processing.
- Four D-pad-cyclable rendering paths: bilinear, EASU-only, AMD FSR 1.0 EASU+RCAS, and experimental mild spatial NR followed by EASU+RCAS.
- Input pipeline: packaged H.264 test clip decoded by Android MediaPlayer to `SurfaceTexture`/external OES, copied into RGBA, then passed through the chosen shader route.
- Packaged synthetic H.264 clips are 640×360, 960×540, 1280×720, and 1920×1080 at 30 fps. The AVD run selected 960×540 and processed at 1920×1080.
- Smoke covered app launch, GLES shader initialization, video playback and frame progression, pause, all mode switches, RCAS 8→9, screenshots, callback FPS, and sparse `glFinish()` completion samples.
- The NR shader is a simple 3×3 edge-aware spatial prefilter. It is not temporal or codec-aware. No compression artifact reduction or deinterlacing is implemented.

## Latest measured evidence

The AVD was API 36, Android TV 1080p, x86, Google SwiftShader OpenGL ES 3.0. This is software-emulator evidence only, not a physical TV or target-GPU result.

- `render_fps=3.5586345` is callback cadence, not screen presentation FPS.
- `render_completion_ms=142.5533 frame=120 mode=fsr1 method=glFinish`; later Bilinear sample was 37.44116 ms at frame 240. These are CPU-submit-to-`glFinish` proxies, not GPU timestamps or end-to-end latency.
- Same-frame 240×1080 crop changed between Bilinear and EASU-only at 10,341/259,200 pixels (3.9896%); Bilinear and FSR1 at 10,342 (3.9900%); Bilinear and FSR1+NR at 10,503 (4.0521%).
- EASU-only vs FSR1 differed at 473 pixels (0.1825%); FSR1 vs FSR1+NR at 7,831 pixels (3.0212%); FSR1 sharpness 8 vs 9 at 125 pixels (0.0482%).
- Two video captures one second apart differed at 40,590/259,200 pixels (15.6597%).
- These pixel differences establish distinct output and motion only. They are not perceptual-quality scores or proof of improved fidelity. The measured software-emulator rate is below 30 fps and is not predictive of hardware performance.
