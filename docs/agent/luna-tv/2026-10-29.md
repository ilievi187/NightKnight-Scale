## Current Android TV implementation

- Foreground Android TV / Google TV GLES demo. This is not transparent or system-wide processing.
- Four D-pad-cyclable rendering paths: bilinear, EASU-only, AMD FSR 1.0 EASU+RCAS, and experimental mild spatial NR followed by EASU+RCAS.
- Input pipeline: packaged H.264 test clip decoded by Android MediaPlayer to `SurfaceTexture`/external OES, copied into RGBA, then passed through the chosen shader route.
- Packaged synthetic H.264 clips are 640×360, 960×540, 1280×720, and 1920×1080 at 30 fps. All four clips from the exact APK built by run `40836334244` were extracted and fully decoded with FFmpeg. The AVD selected 960×540 and processed at 1920×1080.
- The run 40836334244 smoke covered app launch, GLES shader initialization, video playback and frame progression, remote pause and resume with post-resume motion, all mode switches, RCAS 8→9, screenshots, callback FPS, and sparse `glFinish()` completion samples.
- The NR shader is a simple 3×3 edge-aware spatial prefilter. It is not temporal or codec-aware. No compression artifact reduction or deinterlacing is implemented.

## Measured evidence from run 40836334244

The AVD was API 36, Android TV 1080p, x86, Google SwiftShader OpenGL ES 3.0. This is software-emulator evidence only, not a physical TV or target-GPU result.

- `render_fps=4.903206` is callback cadence, not screen presentation FPS. It is below the 30 fps source rate on SwiftShader and is not predictive of hardware performance.
- `render_completion_ms=110.679504 frame=120 mode=fsr1 method=glFinish`; later samples were 156.42589 ms at frame 240 in FSR1+NR and 136.38406 ms at frame 360 in FSR1. These are CPU-submit-to-`glFinish` proxies, not GPU timestamps or end-to-end latency.
- In the paused 240×1080 crop, Bilinear vs EASU-only changed 11,359/259,200 pixels (4.3823%); Bilinear vs FSR1 changed 11,370 (4.3866%); Bilinear vs FSR1+NR changed 11,667 (4.5012%).
- EASU-only vs FSR1 changed 790 pixels (0.3048%); FSR1 vs FSR1+NR changed 8,837 (3.4093%); FSR1 sharpness 8 vs 9 changed 152 (0.0586%).
- Two video captures one second apart differed at 35,192/259,200 pixels (13.5772%); two captures one second apart after remote resume differed at 36,041/259,200 pixels (13.9047%).
- Pixel differences establish distinct output and motion only. They are not perceptual-quality scores or proof of improved fidelity.

## Remaining acceptance gaps

- No physical Android TV/Google TV or target hardware GPU has been tested. This environment has no local Android SDK/Gradle setup or connected device. CI is the reproducible build and AVD path.
- No local user-selected file, tuner, IPTV, or arbitrary app-source integration exists.
- No MediaProjection/VirtualDisplay consent flow, capture lifecycle, separate preview session, or cross-app processing exists. The app does not replace source pixels.
- No OEM/privileged SurfaceControl, SurfaceFlinger, Hardware Composer, or custom firmware integration exists.
- DRM/protected content and capture revocation are untested because capture is not implemented. No bypass exists.
- Named quality presets, adaptive performance policy, compression artifact reduction, and deinterlacing are not implemented.
- Physical remote behavior, target-GPU FPS/latency, quality against a reference corpus, and perceptual improvement remain unverified.
