## Current Android TV implementation

- Foreground Android TV / Google TV GLES demo. This is not transparent or system-wide processing.
- Four D-pad-cyclable rendering paths: bilinear, EASU-only, AMD FSR 1.0 EASU+RCAS, and experimental mild spatial NR followed by EASU+RCAS.
- Input pipeline: packaged H.264 test clip decoded by Android MediaPlayer to `SurfaceTexture`/external OES, copied into RGBA, then passed through the chosen shader route.
- Packaged synthetic H.264 clips are 640×360, 960×540, 1280×720, and 1920×1080 at 30 fps. All four clips from the exact CI APK were extracted and fully decoded with FFmpeg. The AVD selected 960×540 and processed at 1920×1080.
- Smoke covered app launch, GLES shader initialization, video playback and frame progression, remote pause and resume with post-resume frame motion, all mode switches, RCAS 8→9, screenshots, callback FPS, and sparse `glFinish()` completion samples.
- The NR shader is a simple 3×3 edge-aware spatial prefilter. It is not temporal or codec-aware. No compression artifact reduction or deinterlacing is implemented.

## Latest measured evidence

The AVD was API 36, Android TV 1080p, x86, Google SwiftShader OpenGL ES 3.0. This is software-emulator evidence only, not a physical TV or target-GPU result.

- `render_fps=4.780774` is callback cadence, not screen presentation FPS. It is below the 30 fps source rate on SwiftShader and is not predictive of hardware performance.
- `render_completion_ms=175.7694 frame=120 mode=fsr1 method=glFinish`; later EASU-only sample was 114.018845 ms at frame 240. These are CPU-submit-to-`glFinish` proxies, not GPU timestamps or end-to-end latency.
- Same-frame 240×1080 crop changed between Bilinear and EASU-only at 11,599/259,200 pixels (4.4749%); Bilinear and FSR1 at 11,603 (4.4765%); Bilinear and FSR1+NR at 11,695 (4.5120%).
- EASU-only vs FSR1 differed at 655 pixels (0.2527%); FSR1 vs FSR1+NR at 8,965 pixels (3.4587%); FSR1 sharpness 8 vs 9 at 162 pixels (0.0625%).
- Two video captures one second apart before pause differed at 31,221/259,200 pixels (12.0451%); two captures one second apart after remote resume differed at 35,755/259,200 pixels (13.7944%).
- Pixel differences establish distinct output and motion only. They are not perceptual-quality scores or proof of improved fidelity.
